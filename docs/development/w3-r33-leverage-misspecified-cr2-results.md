## Interpretation: a near-zero change in CR2 SE can conceal important CI width changes

R32's ordinary-support pilot had negligible plug-in-vs-oracle CR2 SE changes and identical inclusion decisions. R33 gives the first direct stress evidence that this cannot be extrapolated. Under predictor-dependent Gaussian covariance with extreme leverage, the simulation-known oracle target produced **median conditional Satterthwaite df 1.709 at n24 and 4.428 at n96**, compared with ~10.948 and ~48.841 under ordinary support at the same n. These extremely low effective df imply large, sensitive t critical values even when the estimated contrast SE is numerically stable.

The **deliberately wrong shared-slope working covariance** in the predictor-dependent high-leverage condition gave very different *plug-in* conditional df: median **2.907 at n24** and **12.802 at n96**, versus oracle 1.709/4.428. Median plug-in-to-oracle **CR2 SE ratios were 0.940 at n24 and 0.976 at n96**, but **90%-nominal Satterthwaite interval half-width ratios were 0.683 and 0.837**, respectively. The wrong covariance family could therefore yield a **substantially narrower interval despite superficially close CR2 SEs**. This comparison does not establish undercoverage in a large source population: it exposes model-dependent finite-cluster uncertainty calculations.

## Four original paired inclusion disagreements and source-level uncertainty

There were **four fitted working-family *case* inclusion disagreements** out of 384 paired model cases. They arose only in the original high-leverage **predictor-dependent** source, across **three distinct generating blocks** (n24: block 4 under wrong shared family; n24: block 12 under both matched and wrong family; n96: block 21 under matched family). At n24 extreme-dependent, the oracle included the projected coefficient in **24/24**, the matched plug-in in **23/24**, and wrong-family plug-in in **22/24**. At n96 extreme-dependent, the oracle and wrong-family plug-in each included **23/24**, while the matched plug-in included **24/24**. These changes are directional within specific cells and source blocks, not statistically resolved nominal coverage differences.

For the source-independent extreme leverage design at n24, conventional **t(G−1)** oracle truth inclusion was **19/24**, versus **23/24** using the coefficient-specific conditional Satterthwaite reference—further evidence that the df treatment can materially affect observed inclusion even for a known Gaussian target. All resulting 24-source-cell Wilson intervals remain wide, particularly at 23/24 and 24/24, and eight source support/n combinations are correlated through original nested blocks and reused working families. Do not pool the 384 cases as if independent coverage replications or claim conventional t inference is always inferior.

The estimated REML nuisance variance components remain imperfect, and this pilot retains after-fit simulation-specific matched-family variance boundary diagnostics. Crucially **neither plug-in Satterthwaite df accounts for the uncertainty of estimating covariance parameters on the same participant source data**, so the numerical CR2 identity and 90%-nominal source inclusion are not a valid coverage certification. Deliberately wrong covariance structure is one simplified Gaussian misspecification; additional non-Gaussian joint measurement noise, participant dropout and combined extreme leverage/missingness remain untested.

## Research decision and protected release boundaries

R33 **rejects the presumption that R32's same-interval-decision result automatically transfers to extreme leverage or wrong covariance**. It does not show persistent severe numerical refusal in the new 24-block source: every estimator and R22 CR2 calculation completed. The more substantive obstacle is **inferential sensitivity**—effective degrees of freedom and interval half-widths can shift substantially even when CR2 standard errors appear nearly identical. This mandates a larger independently seeded, failure-inclusive calibration study and then explicit fitted-nuisance-uncertainty handling; it does not warrant loosening adjustment conditioning thresholds or promoting a method.
