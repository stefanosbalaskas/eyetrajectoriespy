# R29 — finite-sample covariance recovery after predictor-spread intervention in an original Gaussian generator

**Research-only independent source study, not nominal inference calibration or an empirical recruitment recommendation.** The previous [R28 original frozen-design information study](w3-r28-predictor-information-results.md) was an *oracle expectation*: it varied participant predictor span while keeping native participant/trial/time designs fixed, discovering information improvements for a predictor-dependent random-slope source but exact invariance for the independent-covariance control. It did not generate new outcomes or fit estimated covariance components. R29 asks whether those information trends extend to **finite-sample, outcome-estimated** random-slope variance under the deliberately restricted source.

## Frozen experiment, prespecified before viewing new source outcomes

Fresh master seed **202610152929**, **12 independent generating blocks** (new relative to R16–R28). Within each, nested n24/n96 participants, ordinary-support independent/dependent source covariance, fixed Legendre-5 mean basis, and participant predictor spread multipliers **0.5, 1, 2 and 4**. Exactly **192 paired counterfactual source cases** (12×2×2×4), but ONLY 12 genuinely independently seeded source blocks. The four multipliers are repeated designs with **identical primitive Gaussian innovations, jittered native timestamps, participant/trial map and 1–3 recorded trials**, not independently observed human data.

For every original generating block, reconstruct new first-dimensional eye trajectory values *from the primitive Gaussian innovations* at the modified x. Recompute **both** the functional fixed-effect mean `mu(t,x)` and (only in the predictor-dependent covariance mode) the primitive participant random-slope term `sigma_slope u_i x_i(1+0.25t)`. The participant intercept, trial Gaussian innovation and sensor innovations are unchanged across paired multipliers. For the independent covariance source, x changes only the fixed mean and the fixed-effect model reparameterization. **The new Gaussian response must never be constructed by simply replacing a predictor column without regenerating the outcome and covariance process.**

Baseline 1× reconstructed observations must match the original source values and native times to machine precision. Under independent covariance, subtracting the correct rebuilt mean must leave *every primitive residual curve unchanged* across multipliers. Under dependent covariance, residual change must agree exactly with the primitive random-slope innovation and x contrast. Tests enforce these contracts.

Fit two methods to precisely the same observed-only counterfactual native curves: original leverage-adjusted NNLS (with disclosed sensor floor) and independently validated Gaussian REML, with only the **matched and prespecified** working covariance family (shared or predictor-scaled slope). No source Gaussian innovation, true parameter or known covariance is passed to the estimators. Recover component error and the *common* post-fit estimate <5% of positive generating slope variance, flag all failures and optimizer nonconvergence, and report each sample size/mode/spread condition separately.

The primary descriptive paired contrast is **2× versus 1× original predictor spread** in absolute relative random-slope variance estimation error, computed within block and bootstrapped with **2,000 whole generating-block resamples** separately by sample size, source mechanism and estimator. The 0.5× and 4× variants are gradient and feasibility diagnostics and do not drive post hoc method selection. Independent-source covariance is a **negative control**: variance component fits should theoretically be invariant under invertible fixed predictor rescaling, up to declared finite numerical optimizer tolerances. Retain any violated invariance as an engineering or estimator warning; never manually delete them.

## Limits

Wider x range may create high leverage and alter the estimand for conditional participant covariance, and a hypothetical fourfold spread may be physically or experimentally infeasible. Gaussian random-slope signal magnitudes are changed by construction, but the *true component variance parameter* is held fixed. A successful reference estimate is still not a robust real-world eye-tracking variance estimator. Sampling noise is paired across multiplier conditions, so 192 cases cannot be treated as independent experiments. At only 12 independent blocks, source-level Monte Carlo evidence remains exploratory.
